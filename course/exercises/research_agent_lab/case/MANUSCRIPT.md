# Commute duration and employment stability: synthetic manuscript

M001 | We study whether longer daily commutes reduce employment stability among urban workers.
M002 | The synthetic sample contains 1,200 workers observed at baseline and twelve months later.
M003 | Commute duration is the self-reported one-way trip in minutes at baseline.
M004 | Employment stability is an indicator for remaining with the same employer at follow-up.
M005 | Longer commutes are associated with lower employment stability in the unadjusted comparison.
M006 | We interpret this pattern as the causal effect of commuting burden on job retention.
M007 | The registered adjustment set contains age, education, household composition, and district.
M008 | Job type was measured but is not included in the registered primary specification.
M009 | Missing follow-up status is treated as non-retention without a sensitivity analysis.
M010 | Results will be presented as a single adjusted risk difference with a 95% interval.
M011 | We conclude that reducing commute time would increase employment stability.
